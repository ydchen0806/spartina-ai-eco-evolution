I. Data Set Descriptors 
   A. Title: Contrasting plant adaptation strategies to latitude in the native and invasive range 
      of Spartina alterniflora: geographic survey (2014) and Common garden (2015-2017)
   B. Accession Number: BOT-GCED-1912
   C. Description 
      1. Originator(s): 
         Name: Steven C. Pennings
         Organization: University of Houston
         Email: spennings@uh.edu
      2. Abstract: We examined trait differences and evolution across geographic clines among 
         continents of the intertidal grass Spartina alterniflora within its invasive and native 
         ranges. Between September and November 2014, we sampled vegetative and reproductive traits 
         in the field at 20 sites over 20° latitude in China (invasive range) and 28 sites over 17° 
         latitude in the US (native range). We grew both Chinese and US plants in a greenhouse 
         common garden for three years (2015 - 2017) to determine if differences in performance of 
         S. alterniflora between the introduced and native ranges were due to genetic differences 
         or differences in abiotic conditions.
      3. Study Type: Directed study
      4. Study Themes: BOT, POP
      5. LTER Core Areas: Primary Production

   D. Keywords: Evolution, introduced species, latitude, phenotypic plasticity, salt marsh

      2. Experimental or Sampling Design 
         a. Design Characteristics: In China we sampled 10 locations ranging from 20.90 °N to 39.05 
            °N in September-November, 2014. To standardize phenology, we started at the 
            southernmost location (Leizhou) when seed set began in late September and ended at the 
            northernmost location (Tanggu) when seeds were being set in early November. In the 
            United States we sampled 14 locations ranging from 27.70 °N to 43.32 °N in October and 
            November 2014. Within each location, we worked at two sites, 2-3 km apart, with a total 
            of 20 sites in China, and 28 sites in the United States. In both geographic ranges, S. 
            alterniflora occupies lower elevations in the intertidal than other plant species. As a 
            practical matter, the easiest way to standardize elevation across all sites was to 
            focus on tall-form S. alterniflora, which we defined as the tallest plants that were 
            growing at each site. These plants were invariably growing low in the marsh or along 
            creek banks, and always occurred in monospecific stands.
         b. Permanent Plots: 
         c. Data Collection Duration and Frequency: At each site, we sampled five 0.5 × 0.5 m 
            quadrats in tall-form S. alterniflora stands, with individual quadrats spaced greater 
            than 30 meters apart. In each quadrat, we measured the height of the three tallest S. 
            alterniflora shoots and counted the number of shoots that were greater than 25 cm tall 
            to calculate shoot density. We haphazardly collected 10 inflorescences within a meter 
            of each quadrat and counted the number of filled seeds in each inflorescence, 
            distinguishing filled from unfilled seeds. We calculated seed set (100*filled 
            seeds/(total seeds)).
            Beginning of Observations: 10-Sep-2014
            End of Observations: 22-Nov-2014
